## 3. Design, corrections and honest caveats

### Lesions (applied at the end of the write phase; the fast field is always zeroed)

| lesion | hologram | fast field | slow field | state left |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HOLO | on | zeroed | zeroed | H only |
| HOLO_SLOW | on | zeroed | kept | H and s |
| SLOW | off | zeroed | kept | s only |
| NULL | zeros | | | negative control (noise only; no simulation needed) |

The fast field is zeroed in every lesion because its linear effect on the probe is removed by the paired baseline subtraction, and because v9 to v11 never separated it from the slow field. Each lesion is followed through delays 0, 250, 500, 1000 and 2000 steps after the write; at each the pooled H and s are captured (361 values over the interior) and the modulation map `0.3 s + 0.5 H` is decoded with the v10 shape targets and regressors, with readout noise at 1 and 4 times the sensor noise (the same realisation for every lesion). Controls: Fourier features (positive), noise-only NULL, and the pooled written map.

### Decision rule (fixed before the reported run)

With h(t) and s(t) the best R-squared of HOLO and SLOW at delay t (counted only if the interval versus NULL is above 0) and T the longest delay: **HOLO_NEVER_ABOVE_FLOOR** if h(t) is below 0.10 at every delay; **PLASTICITY_EXTENDS_MEMORY** if h(T) is at least 0.10 and HOLO beats SLOW at T (paired interval above 0); **SLOW_PERSISTS** otherwise if s(T) is at least 0.10; **BOTH_FADE** otherwise. The usual controls come first. Half-lives are reported descriptively.

### What was tuned, and on which data

* The tuning pass used **seed 1000** with 60 trials. It exercised the plumbing and showed the same qualitative picture (slow field decays slowly, hologram at the floor). At 60 trials the written-map control read 0.24, just under its 0.25 gate (calibrated at 120 trials), so the tuning verdict was STATE_CONTROL_FAILS; at 240 trials the map reaches 0.43 to 0.44. That is a sample-size effect, and the threshold was not changed.
* Seeds **0 and 1** were run once for the reported results. **After that run I added a `--mults` option** (so the noise-free variant could be run) and made the primary-level choice fall back to the first multiplier when 1.0 is absent. I then **re-ran the reported analysis from the cached arrays with the final code and verified that the results are identical** (excluding runtime). The noise-free analysis is **exploratory and post hoc**: it was prompted by the reported result.

### Caveats

1. **The conclusion depends on the noise model.** At the sensor noise level the hologram's part is weak and decays toward the floor; noise-free it does not decay. The noise model is a calibration (v12), not the real probe path.
2. **The hologram's constant noise-free R-squared is true by construction**: after the write, H only decays by the factor `holo_decay` per step, and R-squared is scale-invariant. It shows the pattern is not lost, not that it is readable.
3. **Why the slow field lasts.** Its damping constant suggests a decay of a few hundred steps, but its low-wavenumber components decay slowly (an overdamped mode); I measured the behaviour, not the mechanism.
4. **Linear readouts fail under noise** (R-squared below zero, also on the noise-only NULL); the verdict uses the best readout, so it is unaffected.
5. **Only the slow-versus-hologram comparison is tested.** The fast field, the probe path and the effect of the noise-equivalence assumption are not examined here.

## 8. Limitations

* **One medium, one configuration, two seeds** of 240 trials; the seeds agree.
* **State level only**: no probe was run, so nothing here says what the probe can read at long delays.
* **Delays up to 2000 steps** (about {2000/half_h:.1f} hologram half-lives).
* **The noise model is a calibration**, and the conclusion depends on it (section 3).
* **The hologram's design constants** (`holo_decay` 0.9995, `holo_rate`, `holo_mod`) were not varied.
* **The noise-free analysis is exploratory and post hoc.**

---

## 9. Recommended next step

The hologram's information is intact but its amplitude is too low and its half-life too short to survive the noise; the slow field wins in the range tested. Three cheap-to-moderate steps follow:

1. **Extend the delay analytically.** H evolves only by decay after the write, so its state at any delay is exact without simulation; only the slow field needs simulating. Run the SLOW lesion alone to about 10000 steps (about 15 to 20 minutes) and compute where, in noise-free and noisy terms, the hologram's readable information overtakes the slow field's.
2. **Vary the hologram's design constants** (`holo_decay` 0.9999 or 0.99995, a larger `holo_rate`) in a state-level run, to see whether a hologram with a longer half-life and larger amplitude beats the slow field at realistic noise.
3. **Only then run probe-level retention** (probe at each delay for the three lesions, about 20 minutes extra), to see whether the probe can read the hologram when it is strong enough.
